18 Months at Karl Storz: What I Learned

February 2026

From June 2023 to December 2024, I had the opportunity to work as an Advanced Manufacturing Technology Intern at Karl Storz Imaging in Santa Barbara. Here's what I learned.

The Project: Automating Hospital Testing

Medical devices like endoscopes go through brutal cleaning cycles in hospitals—acids, bases, high-pressure steam. We needed to test how product coatings hold up after hundreds of cycles. The manual process took months per prototype. My job? Automate it.

What I Built

The reprocessing system I integrated combined three subsystems:

  • Cleaning chamber — chemical wash
  • Disinfection chamber — different chemicals
  • Sterilization chamber — autoclave simulation

I programmed a Raspberry Pi and Arduino MEGA to control the entire process, allowing engineers to run overnight tests that would've taken weeks of manual work.

Key Lessons

1. Design of Experiments (DOE) is Powerful
I ran a central composite design to study how temperature, time, and chemical concentration affected coating degradation. This statistical approach saved months of trial-and-error.

2. Integration is Harder Than Building
The individual components existed. Making them work together reliably? That's where 80% of the engineering effort went.

3. Document Everything
When you're building something that others will maintain, your documentation is as important as your code. I learned to write docs as I built, not after.

What's Next

I'm now looking for full-time roles where I can apply these skills in manufacturing, automation, or product development. If you're working on interesting hardware problems, let's connect.
