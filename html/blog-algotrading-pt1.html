Algotrading Parameter Optimization and Selection: Introduction (Pt. 1)

November 16, 2025 Finance & Trading • 3 min read

I won't lie.

As a young adult who just graduated college, there's only four things on my mind: love, health, happiness, and money. Let's just focus on the last item: money; or as some may call this obsession - avarice.

A more specific form of the deadly sin of greed, avarice, I would consider, is pervasive among my generation as a result of this world's rising cost of living and inhospitality to poor early-career financial management.

I certainly will not be the first to tell you about the general public's infatuation with getting rich quickly. Although gambling may be trending down in Las Vegas, prediction markets and sports betting are on the rise, none more so than the infamous US stock market.

Robinhood's increasing prominence (among other brokerage platforms) is a prime example of the astronomical rise in attention the younger generations place on the trading of equities and derivatives in hopes of striking it big.

Now, that's the hard part. Trading isn't easy. It's emotional, volatile, and generally devastating. Beating the broad market (i.e. SPY) is quite difficult, a feat some hedge funds struggle to accomplish.

That's why, after years of manual trading, I want to begin a journey on algorithmic trading. To be totally honest, I've been on this journey for quite a while, but I haven't had the time nor commitment to really do it well (in a smart manner) until now.

As I write out this blog series, I'll take you through my steps as I try to find a set of strategy parameters that are both robust and optimized (for performance, Sharpe ratio, etc.). Testing and coding were carried out by means of Claude Code through the framework of the backtesting.py library.
